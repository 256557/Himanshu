# Compensatory mutation Q1: model answer

## 1. Thermodynamic basis of the loss of function
A destabilising missense mutation lowers the free energy of folding. When stability falls below the margin the protein needs, a larger fraction of molecules is unfolded or aggregated, so the amount of active enzyme in the cell falls even though each folded molecule may still be catalytically competent.

## 2. Global stabilisation by distal substitutions
The most frequent route is a second substitution elsewhere in the protein that raises overall folding stability without contacting the original site. Because stability effects are roughly additive, such a mutation lifts the double mutant back above the stability threshold. Many positions can supply this kind of gain, which makes the route easy to reach by mutation.

## 3. Local structural compensation at the mutated site
A substitution at a residue in contact with the mutated position can restore the specific interaction that was lost. Examples are a complementary change in side-chain volume that fills a cavity or relieves a clash, or a reciprocal charge swap that rebuilds a salt bridge. This route is more constrained than the global one, because only a few neighbouring positions and residues can supply the missing contact.

## 4. Compensation through protein abundance, folding assistance and turnover
Function can also recover without restoring the fold of each molecule:
1. a promoter mutation, a change in mRNA stability or a gene duplication raises the amount of mutant enzyme;
2. increased chaperone activity raises the fraction of molecules that fold correctly and reduces aggregation, without changing their intrinsic stability;
3. loss of a protease or ubiquitin ligase that targets the unstable protein slows its degradation;
4. a compensating change in a binding partner restores an interface that the mutation disturbed.

A suppressor tRNA can also insert a different amino acid at the mutant codon, but it acts at every matching codon in the proteome, so it carries a cost and is rarely the route that persists.

## 5. Mutational target size and the predominance of compensation over reversion
1. A true reversion at the protein level requires one of a few specific nucleotide changes at the original codon, whereas compensation can come from many positions in the same gene and from other genes. The summed mutation rate to compensation is therefore much larger than the rate to reversion.
2. Under strong selection, the first beneficial mutation to arise usually fixes. Because compensatory mutations arise more often, they usually arrive first even when a reversion would give higher fitness.
3. Once a compensator has spread, reverting the original mutation may no longer be beneficial, because the compensator can be neutral or harmful on its own. The lineage then stays on the compensated genotype.

## Conclusion
A second-site mutation restores function mainly by raising overall folding stability, by restoring a lost local contact, or by increasing the amount of correctly folded enzyme through expression, chaperones or reduced degradation. Compensation is observed more often than reversion because its mutational target is much larger, so a compensatory mutation usually arises and fixes before the specific reversion does.
