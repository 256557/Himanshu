Abstract

The interaction of benzimidazole with the biosurfactant rhamnolipid was examined in hydroethanolic media to characterise the physicochemical behaviour of the drug-surfactant system for topical delivery. Density (ρ), surface tension (σ) and contact angle (θ) were measured at 15 °C in 30 % and 50 % (v/v) ethanol over a range of benzimidazole and rhamnolipid concentrations. Surface tension decreased progressively with increasing rhamnolipid concentration, consistent with adsorption of the biosurfactant at the air-liquid interface followed by micelle formation. The critical micelle concentration (CMC) increased with ethanol content and with [benzimidazole concentration], indicating that solvent composition and drug loading both affect aggregation. Contact angle measurements were used to assess the wettability of the benzimidazole-rhamnolipid mixtures. The changes in surface tension and CMC reflect interactions among benzimidazole, rhamnolipid and the hydroethanolic solvent, and provide a basis for adjusting the composition of rhamnolipid-based formulations for topical drug delivery.
