Question. In a large randomly mating population, an autosomal allele t is transmitted by heterozygous males to 80% of their sperm and by heterozygous females to half of their eggs. Males homozygous for t die before reproduction, homozygous females survive with probability 0.5, and other genotypes are unaffected. The sex ratio at fertilisation is equal and there is no mutation. What is the long-term frequency of t among reproducing females? Give four decimal places.

Answer. 0.3700.

Solution. Egg and sperm pools carry t at different frequencies, and the two sexes differ in genotype composition after selection, so female and male adults are tracked separately. With female adult genotype frequencies F and male frequencies M, eggs carry t at F_Tt/2 + F_tt and sperm at 0.8 M_Tt + M_tt. Zygotes form by random union of the two pools. Females retain all TT and Tt zygotes and half of tt; males retain TT and Tt only. Iterating to equilibrium from different starting points gives female genotype frequencies TT 0.3579, Tt 0.5440 and tt 0.0980, so the frequency of t among reproducing females is 0.5440/2 + 0.0980 = 0.3700. The corresponding frequency in males is 0.3016. A single-pool recursion with Hardy-Weinberg zygotes, or one allele frequency shared by both sexes, gives a different value.
